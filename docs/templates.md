# Templates

A template is the shape a spell or a breath covers: a cone, a line, a
circle, and the solids they stand for. This is the design for templates
that tilt as well as turn, stop at what is in their way, and read as
solids on a board seen from above, and for testing a spell aimed at one
creature. Of it, the straight-line test at the end is built, a
template is stopped by it unless it is set to spread, a form that
stands up is lit from above, a cone turns by yaw and tilts by pitch on
the palette's dials, a rectangle stands as a wall or as a beam that
tilts as well, the seat that lays a template has its side view,
every other seat but the DM's has its cards, and a line between two
picked tokens gives their distance and the cover; only range waits, on
sheets.

## Why

The board is a plan seen from above. That works while a template lies
flat. Once a template stands up, a cone beside a dais lights the raised
cells it reaches, and nothing on the board says why those cells and not
their neighbours.

A template also needs a tilt: a dragon overhead breathes down, and a
caster on the floor aims up at a flier. And a cone should not pass
through the wall in front of it.

## How it looks

Lit from above, and gentle. The solid is shaded the way light falling
on it would shade it, so its top, its sides and the line where it meets
the ground read apart, and the map still shows through. Its shadow
falls within its footprint, and the whole footprint is traced in faint
dashes over it, so the dashes sit on the solid's edge and show what a
wall or a step cut off.

## What stops it

A template is stopped by what is in its way unless it is set to spread.
That is the rules' default: a place is caught only if an unblocked
straight line reaches it from the point of origin. Some spells spread
around corners instead, such as the 2014 Fireball.

The point of origin is not in a cone or a line, so a cone, a wall or a
beam never catches the token it is laid from. A circle laid on a token
still catches it, since a sphere's origin is inside it.

So the palette has **Reaches: Line of sight** or **Around corners**.
The rules call the first one line of effect; the palette says line of
sight because people already know the phrase.

- A wall stops it up to the wall's height, which rises from the higher
  of the two cells it stands between.
- A shut door stops it the same way, up to its own height.
- Raised ground stops it as a solid.
- A window counts as a hole, shut or not, though by the rules glass
  stops an effect. The palette says nothing of it; a DM who rules
  otherwise says so at the table, or sets the template to spread.

What is cut off is not part of the template and is not drawn. A flat
form's outline is cut by rays cast from the origin, each stopped where
the line from the origin to the footprint's edge is stopped, with a ray
at each end of a solid edge so a wall's end cuts cleanly. The sweep is
kept apart from the template, so dynamic lighting can take it later. A
solid is cut at each point of the plan by the same test as the cells:
the line from the origin to the point half a cell above the ground
there, so the solid and the caught cells agree, and it climbs onto a
dais where they do.

The ruler's Line mode is not a template. It keeps measuring the way it
does, since a raw measure is still useful.

## Yaw and pitch

A template with a direction turns two ways. **Yaw** turns it about the
vertical; it is the palette's Aim, renamed. **Pitch** tilts it up or
down from level.

Yaw stays on the board: scrolling a template round while laying it
keeps working. Pitch is set in the palette.

The palette shows them as a pair of dials, yaw as a compass seen from
above and pitch as a half circle seen from the side, so each angle is
seen face on. One ball for both would hide every aim below level on
its far side.

The dials draw the template at its width. A cone's wedge opens and
closes with the Spread slider as it moves. A beam draws only the
needle, since 5 ft wide over 100 ft long is a line at that size.

There is no hint under the dials for now. If people find yaw and pitch
confusing, a plain word such as "turn" or "tilt" goes under each.

## Which shapes turn

- **Cone**: yaw and pitch.
- **Rectangle**: stands as a **Wall** or a **Beam**. A wall stands on
  the floor with its height rising from the origin, like a wall of
  fire, and turns by yaw only. A beam is a line in three dimensions
  with its height around that line, like Lightning Bolt's "100 feet
  long and 5 feet wide", and takes yaw and pitch. It catches as a box
  about its line, its width across and its height up, and is drawn as
  the round tube inside that box, so it shades and its lines of equal
  height curve as a cone's do.
- **Sphere, dome, cylinder**: the same every way round, so nothing to
  aim.

## The side view

A tilted template is hard to read from above, so there is a side view.

The seat laying or holding a template with a direction, DM or player,
gets a strip along the board's foot: the slice along its yaw at one
scale both ways, so its angles are true, with the ground, the walls and
raised parts, the tokens near it, and a line per token saying caught or
clear and why, such as "Caught: 30 ft out, on the floor" or "Clear:
raised ground at +10 ft hides it". It is built only from what that seat
sees, and it says caught of exactly the tokens the board catches. It is
there to read the board by, so it has no controls. A circle has no
direction, so it has no strip.

Clear gives the first reason that holds: the token is off the
footprint, beside it, past its end or behind its start; or the
template over its square passes above or below the middle of its cube;
or the template holds it but a wall, a shut door or the ground stops
the line from the origin. The token a template is laid from is neither
caught nor clear: it stands at the origin as the source, with no line.

The strip is where a cone that runs into raised ground is explained:
its side strikes the face and its top spills over the lip, so it
catches only who stands at the edge, and the line to anyone further
back is drawn stopped at the lip. The board stays plain there.

A player whose token another seat's template catches gets a card
instead: a small side view of what matters and one line about what
happened. A card is dealt for each token the side view names, caught or
clear, when the template is let go of. It has the template's name, the
slice closed in on the token and what decides it, with the origin when
it fits, and the token's line. It sits beside its token with a thin line
to it, follows the board as it pans and zooms, and steps aside rather
than cover another card. It stays until it is closed with the X in its
corner or with Escape, which closes the newest first, before anything
else Escape closes. A card closed stays closed until the template is
laid again.

A card is for the player whose token is involved, but tokens have no
owners yet, so until they do every seat but the DM's gets every card,
spectators too. The DM's seat is the one whose role sees what is kept
for the DM, and it already sees all a card could say.

## A spell aimed at one creature

Fire Bolt is "a ranged spell attack against the target", not an area,
so the question is whether the target can be targeted. The caster
picks the target, and the board tests the range and the cover and
draws the line: "Fire Bolt: 70 ft, in range, half cover". It never says
hit. That is a roll against armour, which needs sheets and dice.

Cover is how much of the target a straight line from the attacker
cannot reach: at least half is half cover, at least three quarters is
three-quarters cover, and all of it is total cover, when the target
cannot be targeted at all. Those shares are the system's, in the grid
block of its manifest, and a system that says nothing takes the SRD's.
The straight-line test runs from the middle of the attacker's cube to
fifteen points spread across the target's cube as the attacker sees it,
five heights by three across, and the share it stops is the share
hidden.

Without sheets there is no spell and no range. Until then the line
runs between two tokens picked one after the other, from the first to
the second, and shows the distance and the cover only: "70 ft, half
cover". A template among what is picked, or a third thing, draws none.
The line is marked as a measure is, by the ruler's Seen by as it is
drawn, everyone by default, so a spectator's stays their own. Every
seat that sees it is shown it, judged from what that seat sees, and it
goes when what is picked stops being those two tokens.

Each seat shown the line gets a card beside the target, as a
template's cards sit: the slice along the line closed in on the
target, each line to it drawn as far as it reaches, and what hides it,
such as "From the floor, raised ground at +25 ft hides its lower half".
It closes with its X or with Escape, and opens again when the line is
drawn anew.

## The Select tool

Picking uses a **Select** tool on the rail between Move and the Ruler,
like Photoshop's. It picks tokens and templates, and only what this
seat can see. A click picks the thing under it, and a click on empty
board picks nothing. Dragging draws a box that picks whatever it
touches: a token when the box reaches into its cell, a template when
the box meets any part of its footprint. Shift adds to what is picked,
and a Shift-click on something already picked takes it out. While
Shift is held the cursor is a pointing hand, and the token under it
wears the ring a Shift-click would give it. While Select is held,
dragging on empty board does not pan; the middle button and two fingers
still do. It has no key for now.

Move moves what Select picked, as Photoshop's Move does, and so does a
press with Select on anything picked, unless Shift is held. A drag that
starts on anything picked carries all of it the same whole cells, as a
free placement: each token lands on its cell with no way found, no
movement counted and no wall in the way, and a template's origin moves
as far. A single token's drag keeps all its rules, and a drag in Move
on a token that is not picked moves that token alone and picks it. The
arrow keys step what is picked a cell at a time.

Every picked token wears the dashed ring that turns about a token a
template catches, and one both caught and picked wears one ring. A
token picked alone keeps its own lit ring and the corner handles that
turn it. A picked template's edge turns the brass it wears under the hand,
and a solid's dotted trace turns brass and solid. The template stays on the
board for Select and Move; picking up a pen, choosing Line or Path, or
Escape in the Ruler takes it off. Delete takes off what is picked: the
template, and each token for a seat that may remove one.

## How it is worked out

Exactly, not sampled. A tilted cone is solved at each point of the plan
for the height of its top and its bottom. A beam catches where three
pairs of parallel faces overlap, and its round tube is the same three
pairs with the last drawn in to the tube's side at each point. The beam
is the one form whose solid and squares part, on purpose: a box reads
flat from above, so the tube is drawn and the box catches. Sampling in
slices of height draws contour lines that are not there.

One test serves templates and targeting: whether a straight line in
three dimensions is stopped by a wall to its height, a shut door or
raised ground, with windows as holes. A template runs it from the
origin to each place. Cover runs it from the attacker to points spread
over the target's space, and the share that is stopped is the cover.
